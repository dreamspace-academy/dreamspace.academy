The organisation

The way DreamSpace Academy works rests on four things:

  • people with the skills to solve problems;
  • a place to build;
  • the money, buyers, introductions and continued support that a skill needs before it can earn a living;
  • a network that connects an underserved community to the wider world.

Each page below says how well its part is backed by evidence.

The education system and the MakerSpace

The education system is how people learn the skills, in schools, at partner sites, online and in the academy’s own spaces. The STEAM programmes are one part of it. The MakerSpace is a separate product: a makerspace that local partners can set up, and one more place the courses can run. Both are below.

The education system

The STEAM programmes are one part of what DreamSpace Academy teaches. The academy also runs business and entrepreneurship training, digital security and media literacy training, and training for teachers and people already at work. What the academy teaches.

How learning is checked

A course is completed by showing evidence of what was learned, checked against a published standard. Turning up is not enough. Where someone starts depends on what they can already show, so skills learned anywhere count.

In the STEAM programmes, a stage says what the work is for. Stages have names, not numbers. A level says how deep someone has gone in one subject, and it belongs to a course rather than to a person. The teaching approach explains each of these, and assessment sets out the standard for finished work.

What the research says

Constructionism, a theory of how people learn, makes the case for learning by building things other people can see and use[1, 4]. Reviews that pool a large body of studies find that makerspaces support creativity[24, 25]. Assessing what learners make is also an established practice, with a record of use elsewhere[21]. The reference library lists every source this site draws on.

Learn, Make, Solve, Earn: what the evidence shows

Research on learning by making gives good evidence for the first three: Learn, Make and Solve. It does not for Earn, the step from skills to income. So the academy treats Earn as a hypothesis, something to measure, and does not claim it as a result. See what “Earn” does and does not claim.
